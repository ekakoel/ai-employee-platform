   1: # Company onboarding and identity
   2: 
   3: ## Current model
   4: 
   5: `User.company_id` is the single active company membership in this release.
   6: Changing it or introducing multi-company membership requires a separately
   7: reviewed migration and session contract. Login accepts email and password;
   8: the server resolves exactly one active user and binds the session to that
   9: user's company. Duplicate active legacy email identities fail closed with 409
  10: and require operator reconciliation. The optional legacy `company_id` login
  11: field is ignored, not a tenant selector. `X-User-ID` is not authentication.
  12: 
  13: The only required company field at registration is a unique company name;
  14: the registering owner supplies their name, email, and password. Company
  15: `owner_user_id` is set to the newly created user in the same transaction.
  16: The server assigns the existing `owner` role. Platform admin is never granted.
  17: Email claims reserve normalized addresses and protect concurrent public
  18: registration. Existing addresses are claimed during migration, including
  19: ambiguous legacy duplicates. Registration returns a conflict for existing
  20: addresses or company names. It does not issue a session.
  21: 
  22: ## Verification and delivery
  23: 
  24: New public owners cannot sign in until email verification succeeds. Tokens are
  25: random, single-use, expire after 24 hours, and are stored only as SHA-256
  26: digests. `POST /api/v1/auth/request-verification` is the recovery route after
  27: delivery failure; it returns a generic result for unknown addresses. Invitations
  28: use the same verified email channel and expire after 48 hours. Acceptance
  29: proves email control through the link, creates a `member`, and cannot select
  30: company or role. Invitation links and verification links are delivered via
  31: SMTP-over-TLS when `SMTP_HOST`, `SMTP_FROM`, and `PUBLIC_BASE_URL` are configured.
  32: For deployed environments, the public base URL must be HTTPS. SMTP credentials
  33: come from environment variables. Without delivery, production registration and
  34: invitation issuance return 503; no email is claimed to have been sent. With
  35: `DEBUG=true` in development/test only, response bodies expose tokens for local
  36: testing. Tokens are never stored raw in the database or written to audit logs.
  37: 
  38: SMTP delivery occurs after the database transaction. If delivery fails, the
  39: account remains pending; request verification again. Owners can reissue an
  40: invitation. A mail outbox and endpoint rate limiting remain deployment work;
  41: the public endpoints must be rate-limited at the gateway before exposure.
  42: 
  43: ## Profile and permissions
  44: 
  45: New companies start with `onboarding_status=pending`. Owner can read and PUT
  46: `/api/v1/companies/{company_id}/profile` before completion. A contact email
  47: and industry are required to complete the profile. Company workspace routes
  48: reject pending companies with 403, while `/auth/me` and profile routes remain
  49: available. Legacy companies default to `complete` to preserve operations.
  50: Onboarding state is separate from company activation and email verification.
  51: 
  52: Only the recorded owner may invite, revoke invitations, or appoint/revoke
  53: `ai_admin` through `/api/v1/companies/{company_id}/administrators/{user_id}`.
  54: An `ai_admin` has the existing AI workforce permissions, including agent
  55: management, skills, policy, knowledge, and integrations. An administrator
  56: cannot appoint owners or grant platform privileges. Role changes revoke active
  57: sessions and require a fresh login. The legacy direct-user-creation endpoint
  58: returns 410; employees join by invitation and choose their own password.
  59: AI Employee records remain company-scoped and are governed by existing
  60: permission, tool, and approval controls.
  61: 
  62: ## Migration and cutover
  63: 
  64: `0005_company_onboarding` adds nullable profile/owner fields, email verification,
  65: claims, and invitation records. Existing users remain verified; existing
  66: companies remain complete, preventing a mass logout. The migration does not
  67: guess legacy ownership. Inspect historical company users and audit records,
  68: then assign `owner_user_id` through a reviewed operator procedure before
  69: using owner-only invitation/profile management. Companies with no verified
  70: owner need explicit reconciliation. Duplicate legacy email identities cannot
  71: log in until resolved. Do not run this migration against workspace, staging, or
  72: production data without backup and a reviewed rollout plan. PostgreSQL adds
  73: the owner foreign key; SQLite's additive ALTER column cannot add that FK, so
  74: application-level owner checks remain the boundary there.
  75: 
  76: Run isolated tests before deployment. Production needs SMTP, HTTPS origin,
  77: database migration, gateway rate limits, backup, and operational owner/email
  78: reconciliation. Browser E2E and PostgreSQL concurrency must be validated on
  79: disposable environments; unit tests do not establish either result.
